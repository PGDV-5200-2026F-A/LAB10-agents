3. IMPUTATION METHOD AND JUSTIFICATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method: Multiple Imputation by Chained Equations (MICE) as implemented by
scikit-learn's IterativeImputer (20 iterations, 'descending' order starting from the
most-missing column) with RandomForest regressors (200 trees, min_samples_leaf=5)
as the conditional model. Gender (F/M) was included as an observed predictor;
the other 14 measurement columns (incl. height and stature, both fully observed)
serve as covariates for every imputed value.

Why MICE over simpler choices:
- All missing cells are in columns strongly correlated with fully-observed
  columns (corr with height: span 0.87, foot.length 0.82, hand.length 0.74,
  head.length 0.57, ear.length 0.43, head.breadth 0.39, ear.protrusion 0.29);
  a simple mean/median throws away that information.
- Gender means differ markedly for most measurements (e.g. span M vs F below),
  so gender is used as a predictor instead of one overall mean.
- MICE also uses inter-correlations among the measurements themselves (e.g.
  span vs stature r=0.96), yielding individualized, person-specific estimates
  rather than group-level constants; RandomForest adds robustness to mild
  nonlinearity vs. a plain linear model.
- Imputed values were rounded to the nearest whole unit, matching the resolution
  of the observed data (all observed values are integers).
